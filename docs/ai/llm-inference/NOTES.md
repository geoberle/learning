# Teaching preferences

Gerd requested starting this mission assuming no prior knowledge. Use short lessons and ask for explanations in his own words. Gerd generally prefers diagrams and visualizations alongside explanations; use them to make mechanisms and relationships easier to follow. Pair diagrams throughout the text with an interactive walkthrough at the end. Assuming no prior knowledge should not mean slow pacing: keep straightforward foundations concise and avoid repeating the same operation across explanations, exercises, and walkthroughs. Reserve more repetition and reinforcement for harder concepts. Gerd found the first lesson too simplistic and repetitive; this is pacing feedback, not evidence of mastery. Evidence of understanding is captured in learning-records, starting with his explanation of the first decode step.

Lesson 2 feedback: Gerd reported understanding basically nothing. Definition boxes alone do not introduce unfamiliar concepts adequately. Establish a concrete problem, demonstrate the operation in plain language, then attach its technical name. Introduce one conceptual step at a time; do not compress attention, vectors, Q/K/V, layers, and caching into one lesson. Attention and cache internals remain unestablished.
